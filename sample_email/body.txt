SPECIAL DYNAMIC STRETCHING TO PREPARE FOR SHOOTING\

Most players can’t shoot well not because they lack muscle strength, but because they lack the coordination needed to shoot efficiently. These dynamic stretching exercises, when done every practice and every game as part of your warmup, will produce excellent coordination over the whole season, and will dramatically improve their shooting.

All players lined up side by side.
Carioca – 30 yards.
Walking, raise one arm horizontally, touch the opposite toes to your fingertips at each step. 30 yards.
Walking, raise your knee and touch it to the opposite armpit, with the opposite arm down in front of the knee. 30 yards.
Skipping – high knees, big arm swings. 30 yards, two laps. Make sure they are raising opposite knees and arms.
High Kick – jogging pace, swing and kick an imaginary ball with as much power as possible. Your leg swing should be diagonal, bringing your knee under the opposite armpit. Your leg swing should have as much power as possible, and swing as high as possible, lifting you off the ground. The other exercises build up to this. 30 yards, 3 laps.
 

TECHNICAL POINTS FOR SHOOTING

Approach the ball at an angle, side-on  to the goal.
Plant the non-kicking foot side by side with the ball, not too close, not too far to the side, with the toe pointing towards the target.
Common mistakes: Players step far behind the ball, so they barely reach the ball – no power. They are also forced to plan the kicking foot into the ground to prevent falling, so they do not get the full upward swing, and lose accuracy and power.
Big back swing, with bent knee.
Lock the ankle joint solid for the most power, pointing the toes down.
Strike the ball with the laces, ankle locked.
Snap the knee joint at the moment of contact.
Chest leaning forward at the moment of contact.
Full forward leg swing, so your plant foot comes off the ground.
Swing your leg diagonally, bringing your knee under the opposite armpit.
Swing the kicking leg in a straight line toward the target – that is how you control the direction of the shot.
 

 

OTHER RELEVANT COACHING POINTS

When receiving or passing with the inside of the foot make sure your toes are pointing up (so the ball does not pop up or roll over your foot)
When receiving or passing with the outside of the foot point your toe down (same reason…)
When passing, push through the ball and swing the kicking leg up – do not jab the kicking foot down on the ground.
Before receiving pop away and turn your body to open yourself up to face the field of play, and to face your team mates, so you can pass before you are under pressure. This gives you more time and space.
After passing move to space to give your team mates passing options.
Emphasize the cycle of possession: Pop away, good first touch (one that creates time and space); escape the pressure (to create time and space to pass); accurate pass (to the player with the most time and space)
The cue to make a run is when the player with the ball takes a preparation touch and looks up. The cue to pass is when the runner starts the run. So the run comes first. Otherwise always pass to the feet. When passing to the runner pass so the ball meets the feet as well…
Attackers must always be aware what options the defenders are taking away, and what options they are giving you. Take what they give you.
Start making these coaching points with the warmup exercises, and carry them over throughout the progression.
 

This week we work on the microcycle focused on shooting. Shooting is basically passing into the goal. The same principles apply – you must create time and space in order to shoot.  

Get open – pop away from the defender to create time and space (Questions to ask: Who should pop away? Answer: the player off the ball. How many? Everyone except the player with the ball. How often? Every time the ball moves.)
Good first touch (it should be a small touch, turning away from pressure to create time and space and set up a change in the point of attack)
Escape the pressure from a defender by dribbling to space (the ball attracts defenders, so you must escape to create time and space and make a safe pass). Four ways to do this, depending on how close the defender is: touch to the side, skill move, turn and face away, sole delay.
Accurate pass (No matter what you must pass to the feet. The pass must be on the ground and sharp – no slow balls. Pass to the player with the most time and space).
