IMPORTANT CONSIDERATIONS

It is critical that all players learn the basic skills/moves covered this week.  Don’t expect them to learn all the moves on the first try. It will take time and many practices. These moves should be part of every pre-game warmup. By the end of the season we expect all players to be able to do each move accurately and with speed.
These moves start by pulling the ball backwards or to the side (to get it out of the defender’s reach) and then turning so you are not facing the defender. Players can then dribble away to escape from the defender’s pressure.
In the coming weeks we will continue the focus on dribbling. We will do some technical repetitions, but will work in the context of 1v1, 2v1 and other possession games, where the focus will be dribbling to escape the pressure from a defender, and to create enough time and space to make a safe pass or to get a shot. You should continue to instruct your players on the four stages of the cycle of possession (pop away from the defender to create time and space, good first touch when you receive the ball, escape the pressure to create time and space to pass, accurate pass to the feet, and repeat the entire sequence).
You must demonstrate each move correctly, or play the videos linked to below (a tablet has a nice large screen). The objective is for every player to master these moves and be able to successfully execute them in a game situation. Players should be able to beat a defender 9 out of 10 times in a game situation. So they need to master the mechanics of the move, be able to execute them against a defender, and to time it correctly. They need to be able to change direction and change speed in order to get out of pressure and create time and space for themselves. Of course this will take time and many repetitions.
Players must use the correct technique when dribbling and taking on defenders: take small soft touches with the shoe laces (point your toe straight down). Quick feet and small steps so you can react quickly. Make the defender react to you, not the other way around. Dribble diagonally several yards to get the defender to move laterally, then you can use the defender’s momentum against her or him by changing direction and changing speed.
Players must use the correct technique when passing (contact the ball with the inside of the heel, push through the ball and swing the kicking leg up – do not jab at the ball, or jab your kicking foot into the ground).
Players must use the correct technique when receiving the ball – small touch so you can play it immediately. Use the correct surface of the foot – inside of the heel, or sole of the foot if you are in a tight space.
 

HOW TO BEAT A DEFENDER

When you take on a defender one on one, these are critical points for success:

The approach: dribble with quick small touches, crouched down (ready to spring) and with your eyes ahead.
The move:
Fake in one direction to make the defender commit that way (two or more fakes may be required to make the defender commit)
Change direction in the opposite way
Change speed (accelerate away)
DESCRIPTION OF THE FOUR BASIC MOVES

1: Hook turn: Using the sole of the foot on top of the ball, turn 180 degrees. If you use the right foot turn counterclockwise. If you use the left foot turn clockwise. In other words, keep turning in the direction you are already going as the foot moves toward the ball. The turn should be lightning fast.

2: Pull-push: This move has three parts. First, fake that you will shoot the ball, but instead put your toes on top of the ball. Second, using the sole of the foot pull the ball backwards, and turn your body 90 degrees (to the right if using the right foot, or to the left if using the left foot). Third, push the ball away with the inside of the same foot. This is a 90 degree move, drawing the letter “L”.

3: Pull-cut: Similar to the pull-push, this has three parts. First, fake the shot. Second, using the bottom of the toes (sole) pull the ball back behind the standing leg. Third, using the inside of the same foot cut the ball 90 degrees behind the standing leg.

4: Scissors: Take a leap to the side moving the foot in front of the ball. Push the ball in the opposite directions using the outside of the other foot.

5. Maradona: This combines the hood turn, then the drag-back. First do a hook turn with one foot. Second, do a drag back with the other foot. Keep spinning in the direction you are already going. Do the move in both directions.

LEAGUE OBJECTIVES: The two main objectives of the Academy league are fun and development. Therefore:

Fun: Your personality, mood, body language, and demeanor are key. Have fun, interact with the players, be positive and kind, be a good listener. Recognize and reward effort rather than game results.
Development: Be prepared, give them specific instructions, help them learn their positions during the game’s four moments (attack, defense, transition to defense, transition to attack). Stay active.
Be an active (but positive) coach – give players feedback and instructions while they play, before they substitute in, and after they substitute out. Focus on two or three important things. Too much information is confusing and leads to mental shut-down.
